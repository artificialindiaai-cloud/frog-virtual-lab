Virtual Frog Lab

Amphibian heart and skeletal muscle experiments

Virtual Frog Lab is an interactive simulation of the classic frog heart and gastrocnemius–sciatic muscle experiments from the physiology practical. Students record live tracings, change drugs, temperature, stimulus and load, and see the results instantly, without using animals. It runs on a laptop, tablet or phone.

Pick an experiment. Use the speed slider to slow the graph down and study each wave.
Cardiogram: the third line is what a single lever on the frog heart records: a small hump (atrial systole) followed by the large ventricular wave.
Stannius ligatures: Ligature 1 (sino-atrial junction): atria and ventricle both stop in diastole, then resume slowly under the AV pacemaker. Ligature 2 (AV groove): atria keep beating at their rhythm, the ventricle stops, and later beats at its own very slow rhythm.
Nerve stimulation: Vagosympathetic trunk: after a latent period the heart slows or stops in diastole (vagus), then escapes. After stimulation it beats faster and stronger (sympathetic after-effect). After atropine, only the acceleration is seen. White crescentic line (sino-atrial junction): no latent period, atria and ventricle stop in diastole (vagal endings and ganglia). Atropine blocks it.
Properties of extrasystole: (1) Occurs only if the stimulus falls in diastole (long refractory period in systole). (2) Weaker when earlier. (3) Followed by a compensatory pause: coupling interval + pause = 2 normal cycles. (4) The next beat is stronger (post-extrasystolic potentiation). (5) Atria are unaffected. (6) Cardiac muscle cannot be tetanised.
Drug pearls: Cold ↓rate. Warm ↑rate, ↓force. Adrenaline ↑rate and force. ACh ↓rate, ↓force, ↑A–V delay. Atropine blocks muscarinic receptors. Nicotine: first slowing (vagal ganglia), then acceleration. Excess NaCl antagonises Ca²⁺ (diastolic stop). Ca²⁺ → systolic arrest. K⁺ → diastolic arrest.

Genesis of tetanus: skeletal muscle has a very short refractory period (about 3 ms) compared with the twitch (about 150 ms). When stimuli come faster than the muscle can relax, each new twitch rides on the previous one (summation) because Ca²⁺ stays high. At low frequency you see incomplete tetanus (wavy plateau, partial relaxation). At high frequency the waves fuse into complete tetanus (smooth plateau, force about 3–4× a twitch). If it goes on, force falls because of fatigue. Cooling lowers the frequency needed; warming raises it.
Pearls: Two stimuli: second ignored in the absolute refractory period (about 3 ms), summation if it falls during the first twitch, separate twitches if later. After-load: the muscle first contracts isometrically until tension equals the load, so latent period rises and shortening falls as load rises. Free load stretches the muscle first and changes initial length. Work = load × shortening, so it is zero at no load and zero at maximal load. Fatigue is first at the neuromuscular junction (ACh depletion), then in the muscle (ATP/PCr fall, lactic acid, K⁺ rise).
